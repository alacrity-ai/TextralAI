# The Pharos of Alexandria

The Pharos was a lighthouse on the small island of Pharos at the
mouth of Alexandria's harbor, completed around 280 BCE under
Ptolemy II. At an estimated 100 meters tall, it was for many
centuries the tallest man-made structure in the world after the
Great Pyramid.

Its architectural genius was practical: the structure was built in
three stepped tiers — square base, octagonal middle, cylindrical
top — each narrower than the last, distributing weight in a way
that resisted the soft alluvial soil and the regular Mediterranean
storms. The mirror at its summit is variously described as
polished bronze or burnished brass; whatever the metal, the
testimony is consistent that the reflected fire could be seen from
roughly 50 kilometers offshore.

The Pharos collapsed in stages. A series of earthquakes between
956 CE and 1323 CE first damaged the upper structure, then the
middle tier, then finally toppled the base. The Mamluk Sultan
Qaitbay built a fortress on the same site in 1480, incorporating
the lighthouse's surviving foundation stones into the citadel that
still stands.

What we know of the Pharos comes from three sources: descriptions
by ancient geographers (Strabo, Pliny, Lucian); Arabic accounts of
the lighthouse during the medieval period when it was still
partially standing; and the underwater archaeology of the harbor
floor, which has recovered hundreds of cut blocks and statuary
pieces since the 1990s. The harbor itself is now a protected
archaeological site.
