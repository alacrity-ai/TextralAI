# The Lighthouse Keeper of Pernell's Cove

Iola Brennick had kept the lamp at Pernell's Cove for forty-three
winters. The lighthouse, a slate-grey tower stitched into the cliff
by some long-dead engineer named Ferren Voss, had outlasted every
captain who cursed it and every fisherman who blessed it.

Each evening at the seventh bell, Iola climbed the one hundred and
sixty-two iron steps to trim the wick. The lamp burned a particular
oil — pressed from the seeds of the alder-thistle, harvested in the
salt marshes south of the village of Marrowend. No other oil would
hold a flame against the cove's cold wind.

The autumn of her forty-third winter, a stranger arrived in
Marrowend on a fishing skiff called *Ember's Toll*. He gave his name
as Cassen Thrace and asked, in a quiet voice, whether the keeper of
the lighthouse still lived. The villagers pointed up the cliff path.

Iola met him at the door of the keeper's cottage. He carried a
sealed letter and a copper compass that had stopped working in
1898. The compass, he said, had belonged to his grandfather — a
sailor lost in the great gale of that same year. The letter was
from a maritime archive in the city of Halverick, two hundred miles
up the coast, asking Iola to confirm the names of three vessels
that had wrecked on the cove's rocks during her tenure.

She invited him inside, poured tea, and lit the lamp. Outside, the
seventh bell rang clear across the water.
